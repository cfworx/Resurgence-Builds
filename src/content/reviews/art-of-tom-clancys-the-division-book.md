**Checked November 15, 2026.** This is the book for people who stop in the middle of a firefight to look at the street. It collects the concept art for the first Division game in a large hardcover, with the artists explaining what they were going for. It has no story and almost no lore, and it does not need them.

## What it is

Titan Books published it in March 2016, alongside the first game. The publisher's description is over 300 images across 192 pages: early sketches, finished concept paintings and commentary from the artists.

![The front cover of The Art of Tom Clancy's The Division: a Division agent seen from behind, standing in a snowy New York street](/images/reviews/art-of-the-division-front-cover.webp "The front cover.")
![The back cover of The Art of Tom Clancy's The Division, showing a burning building at night above the blurb, a barcode and the Ubisoft logo](/images/reviews/art-of-the-division-back-cover.webp "The back, with the Titan Books price label.")

## Inside our copy

![A two-page spread of concept art from The Art of Tom Clancy's The Division: two agents in a tiled subway passage hung with plastic sheeting and lit by blue lamps](/images/reviews/art-of-the-division-subway-concept-art.webp "A subway passage across two full pages.")

The pictures get the space. Some spreads are a single painting across both pages with a short note in the corner. Others set a main image beside smaller studies of the same place.

![A spread titled Hudson Refugee Camp showing a snow-covered train yard with stranded subway cars and a camp built around them, next to smaller studies](/images/reviews/art-of-the-division-hudson-refugee-camp.webp "The Hudson Refugee Camp pages: a train yard turned into a camp, with a note quoting one of the artists by name.")

The text does two jobs. A short paragraph says what the location is in the game's world, and a caption quotes the artist on how the scene was built. If you like knowing why a picture works, those captions are the reason to own the book and not just look at screenshots.

## What it does not cover

It is the 2016 game and nothing else. Washington, D.C. and The Division 2 are not in it, and neither is Resurgence, which came ten years later. We have not found a standalone art book for either of those games. A book of lithographs for The Division 2 exists, but it only came inside that game's collector's editions.

Resurgence players will still recognize the place. Ubisoft's page for Resurgence says the game is set in New York City, and this book is that city, snowed in and emptied out, as the first game's artists imagined it.
