**Tactical Augmentation Modules** (TAMs, or "augments" if you're in a hurry) are the upgrade most new level 40 agents don't notice until they get flattened in the Dark Zone by someone with the same gear and twice the Health. They're long-term upgrades applied through the **Armory**, they exist for weapons, gear and every exotic, and the module for a gun is worth it even before you find the perfect copy of that gun.

This guide sorts what Ubisoft has actually said (patch notes, its drop-rate table, its news posts) from what creators have read off their own screens, and labels which is which.

> [!INFO] Update (October 2026)
> Rewritten for Season 2 Phase 2. Added the Diamondback module, the Exotic Sealed Cache odds for every exotic TAM, and October readings from TikTokAndy's builds. Ubisoft extended Season 2 Phase 2 on October 21, so these sources are still current.

## What a Tactical Augmentation Module does

Ubisoft described the system before launch, in its June 2023 preview: the Armory "works like a catalog of all the weapons and gear in the game," and it lets you use Tactical Augmentation Modules "to improve a specific model of weapon." The key line: "any upgrade you apply here will apply to all of the, say, M700 marksman rifles you find or loot."

That's the part that changes how you farm. A module doesn't upgrade one lucky drop. It upgrades the **model**, so when a better-rolled copy of the same gun drops later, the upgrade is already there. We can't find a 2026 post that restates the rule, and nothing creators have shown since contradicts it. When TikTokAndy slotted his first gear module in September, he wondered aloud whether it applied to all his Fury Strike chests, and guessed that it did.

There are three kinds, going by Ubisoft's patch notes:

| Kind | What it upgrades | Where Ubisoft mentions it |
|---|---|---|
| **Weapon** TAMs | A weapon model, for example a shotgun module from a Dark Zone mission | Season 1 Phase 2 notes; patch 1.1.2.2 (the "Ringleaders" shotgun reward fix) |
| **Gear** TAMs | Gear | Season 1 Phase 2 notes (Clan Vendor stock) |
| **Exotic** TAMs | One exotic: Lady Death, Warlord, Strawberry Milkshake, Scorpio, The Bighorn, The Diamondback, plus an unannounced Deadlock in the drop table | Pass rewards, Dark Zone vendor, Exotic Sealed Caches |

## What they add: creator readings

Ubisoft doesn't publish a table of module stages, so everything in this section is what creators read off their own Armory screens. Values differ by weapon model, so treat them as examples, not rules.

### Gear modules

| Stage | Reading | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 module | +10% total Health | Died Again Gaming, May 28 (a clan-vendor gear module) |
| 2 | 40% Health and Armor | Died Again Gaming, May 28 |
| 3 | 70% Health and Armor | Died Again Gaming, May 28 |
| 4 | 100% Health and Armor | Died Again Gaming, May 28; TikTokAndy, September 16 ("if you level it up to 4") |

TikTokAndy's October readings mostly line up: a vest with "all the tactical augmentations" for **+100% Health and Armor** (October 6), and on his October 27 Lightweight M4 build, four modules on the chest piece plus a few Armor mods put him at about **10,000 Armor**. Two wrinkles: on October 10 he described two modules on a gear piece as "50 health and armor," which doesn't match the stage table above, and on October 6 "one augmentation" on his mask read **+5% Damage Reduction**, not Health (he doesn't say whether that one is a module or a manual). So gear pieces may not all share one track. Trust your own screen.

TikTokAndy's back-of-the-envelope math, the day after his first trip into the level 40 Dark Zone: +100% on each of six pieces is "an extra 600%." Ubisoft hasn't said how the bonuses stack, so we wouldn't put that number on a T-shirt, but the direction is right: maxed gear modules are the reason veterans feel unkillable.

### Weapon modules

| Weapon | What the modules gave | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The Bighorn | +20% damage from the first module | TikTokAndy, September 16 |
| Enhanced ACR | Two modules: +20% damage, +18% damage to elites, +20% accuracy | TikTokAndy, September 19 |
| Double Barrel | +12% Critical Hit Chance from the first module; +40% damage once he slotted his third | TikTokAndy, October 6 and October 2 |
| An SMG | First stage: Magazine Size +45 and vertical stability +40, as he read them, "and so on and so forth" from there | Died Again Gaming, May 28 |
| Another weapon | First stage: +30% Signature Ability Charge Efficiency, then damage | Died Again Gaming, May 28 |
| MP7 | +10% Skill Intensity from one module | TikTokAndy, October 10 |

Two patterns stand out. First, each model has its own track: on his Aegis healer, where the MP7 is his primary for its Enhancement Trade II and Adaptation II talents, TikTokAndy points out as a fun fact that one module on it adds +10% Skill Intensity. Second, the big damage stages don't always come first. On the Bighorn, his first module gave +20% damage and the screen showed +40% for the next stage, which he found out "you need two to activate." On the Double Barrel, the first module gave +12% Critical Hit Chance and the +40% damage arrived with his third. Same creator, two guns, two tracks, so read the Armory screen for your own weapon before you spend.

## Where to get them

| Source | What you get | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Clan Vendor** | Weapon and gear modules, bought with clan credits. Season 1 Phase 2 added "one more copy of each regular Weapon and Gear Tactical Augmentation Module." | Official (notes) |
| **Dark Zone vendor** | A **weekly Random Tactical Augmentation Module Crate** (added Season 1 Phase 2), exotic modules (Strawberry Milkshake since Season 1 Phase 2, The Bighorn since Season 2 Phase 1), an Exotic Weapon Tactical Augmentations Cache (patch 1.2.1 fixed its translation) and tactical augmentation manuals | Official for the crate, the named modules and the exotic cache; creator reading for the manuals (TikTokAndy, September 16, who pulled a Bighorn module from a random exotic cache) |
| **Classified Ops Pass** | The exotic's module: Scorpio (free track, Season 1 Phase 3), The Bighorn (free track, Season 2 Phase 1). TikTokAndy also pointed to augmentation mods in the last levels of a pass. | Official (Ubisoft News) |
| **Events** | The Diamondback module was a Season 2 Phase 2 event reward; the August Bighorn community challenge promised "an extra Tactical Augmentation for all participants" | Official (Ubisoft News, Steam) |
| **Exotic Sealed Caches** | Every exotic's module at **1.43%** per cache, in the Tier A pool | Official (Help Center drop table) |
| **Encrypted Data store** | An **Exotic TAM Selection Cache** for 1,200 Encrypted Data, where you pick the gun | Creator reading (Died Again Gaming, June 24) |
| **Dark Zone quests** | Tactical Augmentation rewards: patch 1.1.2.2 fixed the Shotgun module from the "Ringleaders" mission not being granted, and patch 1.2.1 still listed another Dark Zone quest's module as unclaimable | Official (notes) |

### What a clan vendor module costs

In May, Died Again Gaming estimated that one weapon module cost about **150,000 clan credits**, roughly **three weeks** of clan supplies. His tips for getting there faster: donating clan supplies pays double in credits (700 supplies became 1,400 credits on his screen), the weekly base supply cap is 20,000, and four Clan Hostile Nest Investigations at 5,000 supplies each, by his count, fill it. Season 2 may have changed the prices, so check the vendor. Our [clan guide](/guides/division-resurgence-clan-guide/) covers the investigations.

### Exotic modules from Exotic Sealed Caches

Ubisoft's drop table for the Exotic Cache puts each exotic's module in Tier A:

| Tier | Tier chance | Items | Each item |
|---|---|---|---|
| S | 2.00% (guaranteed within 50) | Seven exotic weapon caches | 0.29% |
| **A** | **10.00% (guaranteed within 10)** | **Seven exotic Tactical Augmentation Modules** | **1.43%** |
| B | 88.00% | Exotic mod selections, recalibration booklets and manuals | 4.19% |

So the Tier A pity hands you *a* module at least every 10 caches, with a 1-in-7 shot that it's the one you wanted. Full odds and the math for chasing a specific exotic are in our [Exotic Sealed Caches guide](/guides/exotic-sealed-caches/) and the [drop rates database](/database/drop-rates/).

### Unwanted modules aren't worthless

Since Season 1 Phase 2, Weapon, Gear and Exotic modules **dismantle into Encrypted Data** instead of Clan Credits. If the weekly crate hands you your fifth shotgun module for a build that doesn't own a shotgun, it still pays toward the Encrypted Data store.

## Tactical augmentation manuals

Creators talk about manuals as their own item. TikTokAndy, on September 19, after realizing he had never slotted his: one would have added **+10% Health**, "you could change these for free," and "those tactical augmentation manuals are the easiest stat gain you're ever gonna get." His lesson: every time you change a piece of gear, check that your manuals came along. He also lists them among the Dark Zone vendor's stock, and on October 27 he got one as a Lone Wolf Challenge floor reward.

Ubisoft's notes don't describe manuals separately, so we can't tell you the full rules, only what creators show.

## What to augment first

Creator consensus, from the builds on this site:

1. **Your main weapon's model.** Because modules apply to the model, augmenting the gun you'll keep using pays off on every future copy. TikTokAndy's Bighorn felt different with one module ("20% extra damage is crazy").
2. **The chest piece.** TikTokAndy's October builds (October 6, 10 and 27) all max the chest first. On his Lightweight M4 build, four chest modules plus Armor mods put him at about 10,000 Armor, and full Precise Support II stacks then take it to about 14,000.
3. **The rest of the gear, for Health and Armor.** After his build went from fine in the level 39 Dark Zone to "it ain't the same" in the level 40 one, TikTokAndy named modules "the most important thing" to fix.
4. **Check odd tracks before you buy.** Not every model's first module is damage: the MP7's is Skill Intensity on TikTokAndy's Aegis healer, and one augmentation on his mask gave Damage Reduction.

Clan credits are the slowest but steadiest route, which is why GGReborn put "not focusing on clan credits" on his list of mistakes, calling the modules "very very important for your build."
